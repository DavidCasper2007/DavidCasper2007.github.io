Prestige

Prestige was Rice Eclipse’s first successful two-stage rocket. I worked on its pre-flight assembly and its launch operations, and on ground separation tests of its recovery system in November 2025. The video at the top of this page is one of those tests.

Ground separation tests

A recovery system only works if the ejection charge separates the airframe every time. Too little black powder and the shear pins hold. Too much and the charge can damage the rocket. So each charge is proven on the ground before flight: the rocket is laid out fully assembled, the charge is fired, and the sections have to separate cleanly.

The charges were sized with the team’s MATLAB calculator. It first finds the pressure that breaks the nylon shear pins, from the number of pins n, their shear strength τ and their cross-section.

P=n⁢τ⁢ApinAbulkhead

It then uses the ideal gas law to turn that pressure and the volume of the parachute bay into a mass of black powder, with safety factors for the primary and backup charges.

m=P⁢VR⁢T
